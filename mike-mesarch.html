Story Assist

The Day He Was Born

Saturday, April 21, 1951. Harry Truman was president. The country was still talking about his controversial removal of General Douglas MacArthur from Korean War command, ten days earlier. That same day, the Rochester Royals beat the New York Knicks to win the NBA Finals. On the radio, "How High the Moon" by Les Paul and Mary Ford was the country's best-selling record. The Korean War was in its second year. It was the defining story of the era Mike was born into.

We don't know what his family talked about that day. We only know the world that was waiting for him.

Story Assist

An Army Rebuilding Itself

The draft had ended six years earlier, in 1973, and the Army was still in the middle of reinventing itself as an all-volunteer force. Officers commissioned in that era, like Mike, were part of the generation the Army needed to prove the new model could work.

We don't know how often that shift came up for Mike directly. We only know it shaped the Army he was commissioned into.

1979–94

Army Reserve

Serves with the 187th Infantry, retiring a Major after twenty years of service.

Story Assist

Ready for a War That Never Came

Under Cold War planning, the 187th stood ready to deploy to Iceland if war with the Soviet Union ever came. It never did. The brigade trained for two decades for a mission that stayed hypothetical, until it was inactivated in 1994. That was the same year Mike's own service came to a close.

We don't know how often Iceland came up at drill. We only know the possibility was always there, year after year.

Story Assist

The World They Met In

1984 was a landmark year. The Summer Olympics came to Los Angeles that July. It was the first Games held on U.S. soil since 1932. Ronald Reagan was re-elected president that November in one of the largest landslides in U.S. history. Apple had just introduced the Macintosh computer to the world in January, and MTV was at the height of its cultural power. It was the year of Prince's Purple Rain, Bruce Springsteen's Born in the U.S.A., and Cyndi Lauper's Girls Just Want to Have Fun.

We don't know the day Mike and Joan actually met, or what was playing when they did. We only know this is the world they found each other in.

The World That Day

Mike's military honors were held during one of the hardest stretches of the COVID-19 pandemic, a time when gatherings everywhere were smaller, quieter, and harder to hold than they should have been. That same week, the country was watching the first-ever fully virtual Democratic National Convention, held remotely because of the very pandemic reshaping how families everywhere, including this one, were able to come together to say goodbye.

We don't know exactly what that day looked like for those who were there. We only know it took place inside a moment the whole country was living through, unlike any before it.
