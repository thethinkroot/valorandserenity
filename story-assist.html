Story Assist

What It Is, and Why It's Worth Using

A closer look at the optional feature that helps families turn memories into a finished story, grounded in real history.

The Problem It Solves

Most of what we know about someone we love was never written down. It lived in conversation. A story told once at a holiday dinner. A detail mentioned in passing that nobody thought to ask more about. When someone is gone, those details go with them unless someone else remembers to ask, and remembers to write it down.

Story Assist exists because staring at a blank box that says "tell their story" is one of the hardest ways to start. Most people freeze. Not because they have nothing to say, but because they have too much, and no place to begin.

What It Actually Does

Story Assist Has Three Specific Jobs

It asks the right questions.

Not a generic list. Questions built around what you have already told us about your veteran. If you mentioned his unit, it might ask if he ever talked about a specific training base. If you mentioned an era, it might ask about something that would have shaped him at the time. The goal is to jog a memory you did not know you still had.

It shapes what you already have into a finished story.

A few lines, a half remembered story, a line from an old letter. Story Assist turns fragments into something readable, in your own words, not replaced by anything artificial. It never adds a detail you did not give it.

It places every major milestone inside real history.

Not just their service. The day they were born. The day they married. The day they came home. For any date you add, Story Assist can show you who was president, what song was on the radio, what the country was talking about that day. Using verified records, not guesses. If a detail is not reliably documented for that exact day, it says so honestly rather than offering something that sounds right but is not confirmed. The goal is a real, sourced piece of context that might bring back a memory. Not a plausible-sounding fact standing in for a real one.

Why This Is Worth the Price

Story Assist costs $99, once. Not a subscription.

Compare that to the alternatives. A professional biographer or videographer for a similar project can run into the hundreds or low thousands of dollars, and takes weeks. A blank page and good intentions can take even longer, and often ends with nothing written down at all.

There is also this. A basic tribute is free for every family, and it always will be. That only works because families who choose Story Assist help cover the cost of keeping it free for everyone else. When you use Story Assist, you are not just getting help with your own story. You are helping make sure the next family, one that may not be able to pay for anything at all, still gets a place to remember their veteran.

Story Assist is built to close the exact gap that costs families the most: the memories that were never asked about while there was still time. That is not a feature. That is the actual value.

What It Will Never Do

Story Assist works with your words and your memories only. It never invents a detail you did not provide. It never states a personal fact about your veteran as certain unless you confirmed it. It never generates a voice, an image, or a video of someone who is gone. What you type is used only to help shape your story, then discarded. It is never stored to train AI, by us or by anyone else.
