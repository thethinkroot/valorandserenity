Help for Families at This Time

The Practical Part, So You Have More Room for Everything Else

Losing a veteran comes with grief and, often at the same time, a list of unfamiliar steps. This page is here to help you get oriented, clearly and honestly, with no assumptions and nothing made up.

Valor and Serenity is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to the Department of Veterans Affairs, any hospice organization, or We Honor Veterans. Everything below is offered as general information to help you get oriented. Benefit amounts, forms, and eligibility rules can and do change. Always confirm current details directly at VA.gov, or with a Veteran Service Officer, before relying on anything here.

The First Few Days

A few things tend to matter most right away, before anything else can move forward.

  • Contact the funeral home you're working with, they typically help coordinate a burial flag and can guide you through the immediate arrangements.
  • Locate the veteran's DD-214 (their discharge paperwork), if you can find it. Nearly every VA benefit claim that follows will ask for it.
  • Order several certified copies of the death certificate, more than you think you'll need. Almost every claim, account, and benefit office will each want their own original copy.

VA Benefits You May Be Eligible For

This is general information, not a determination of what you specifically qualify for. The VA's own site is the authoritative source for eligibility and current details.

Burial Flag

An American flag to drape the casket or accompany the urn, provided for eligible veterans, typically arranged through the funeral home.

Presidential Memorial Certificate

An engraved paper certificate signed by the sitting President, honoring the veteran's service. You can apply online at VA.gov, by mail using VA Form 40-0247, by fax, or in person at a VA regional office. Multiple copies can be requested for family members.

Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)

A tax-free monthly payment for a surviving spouse, dependent child, or parent, when the veteran's death was caused by a service-connected condition, or occurred while on active duty.

The base monthly rate for a surviving spouse was reported at $1,699.36 for 2026. Rates change over time, confirm the current figure directly at VA.gov before relying on it.

Burial Allowance

Helps cover burial and funeral costs for an eligible veteran. In many cases, a surviving spouse of record is paid this automatically once the VA is notified of the death, with no separate claim required.

National Cemetery Burial

Eligible veterans, and in some cases their spouse or dependent children, may be buried in a VA national cemetery, which includes the gravesite, a government headstone or marker, and perpetual care.

There are other benefits worth asking about too, including survivor pension, Social Security survivor payments, and any life insurance the veteran held. The full, current list lives at VA.gov, and a Veteran Service Officer can walk through what applies to your specific situation, at no cost to you.

A Veteran Service Officer (VSO) is a trained advocate who can help you understand and file for these benefits, entirely free. If you don't already have one, your county or state veterans affairs office can connect you with one near you.

A Veteran Pinning Ceremony

If your veteran was in hospice care, they may have had, or still could have, a Pinning Ceremony: a quiet, meaningful moment where hospice staff or a fellow veteran volunteer present a certificate and a flag pin, and formally thank them for their service, often with family present. It's a real, established part of many hospice programs' care for veterans, usually offered at no extra cost. If this is still ahead of you, it's worth asking your hospice team whether they offer one.
